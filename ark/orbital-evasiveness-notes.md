# The Minimum-Orbital Function μ(n): Limits and Arithmetic Content of the Group-Theoretic Approach to Sparse Evasiveness

*Working notes, July 2026 (updated July 23). Status: conjectural asymptotic framework with proof sketches; two load-bearing structure claims (marked ★) are unproven. Sections 7–8 report exact machine computations at small composite n (code and logs accompany this note). Intended as a starting point for others.*

## 1. Background and the abstracted problem

Babai–Banerjee–Kulkarni–Naik (BBKN, arXiv:1001.4829) prove evasiveness of sparse monotone graph properties via the Kahn–Saks–Sturtevant (KSS) topological method: find a group Γ ≤ Sym([n]) satisfying **Oliver's condition** (a chain Γ₂ ◁ Γ₁ ◁ Γ with Γ₂ a p-group, Γ₁/Γ₂ cyclic, Γ/Γ₁ a q-group) such that every orbit of Γ on unordered pairs ("u-orbital") is larger than the maximum edge count of the property. Then any invariant graph in the property is empty, the fixed-point complex is {∅}, χ = 0 ≠ 1 mod q, contradicting Oliver's fixed-point theorem + the KSS contractibility lemma.

The entire sparse-regime method is thus governed by one extremal function:

> **Definition.** μ(n) := max { m\*(Γ) : Γ ≤ Sym([n]) satisfies Oliver's condition }, where m\*(Γ) is the minimum u-orbital size of Γ.

> **Meta-theorem (implicit in BBKN).** If a nontrivial monotone property P holds only for graphs with < μ(n) edges, then P is evasive at n. Hence f(n) = o(μ(n)) ⟹ f-sparse monotone properties are eventually evasive.

BBKN establish μ(n) = Ω(n log n) unconditionally (via Vinogradov/Haselgrove three-primes), Ω(n^{5/4−ε}) under ERH — subsequently made unconditional by Shparlinski (TCS 2015, arXiv:1304.0188) via average-type results on linear equations in primes — and Ω(n^{3/2−ε}) under Chowla's conjecture on least Dirichlet primes.

**The questions addressed here.** (1) Is n^{3/2} an intrinsic ceiling on μ, or an artifact of proof technique? (2) If the latter, how far can μ be pushed under believed conjectures, and where does the method terminally cap? (3) Does large μ(n) conversely *imply* number-theoretic statements — i.e., is μ an encoding of prime distribution rather than a consumer of it?

Summary of answers: (1) technique artifact — the 3/2 exponent marks the provable/conjectural boundary, not a group-theoretic ceiling; (2) exponent 2 with an explicit constant δ₀ ≈ 1/12 of n², conditional on a single Bateman–Horn-type conjecture, and the terminal cap is a constant c\* < 1/2 forced by group theory (Zassenhaus), not by number theory; (3) yes, conjecturally μ(n) ≥ n^{1+θ−o(1)} is *equivalent* to a θ-graded family of binary-Goldbach-with-side-condition statements.

## 2. Structure reduction ★

The engine converting μ into arithmetic:

> **Claim 2.1 (extremal form of Oliver groups with large min-orbital) ★.** Suppose Γ satisfies Oliver's condition and m\*(Γ) = M > n^{3/2+ε}. Then, up to passing to a subgroup/quotient achieving the same bound, Γ has the form
>
> Γ ≅ [ (𝔽_{p^α}^+)^k ⋊ (C_d × Δ_k) ] × ∏_{j=1}^{t} ( 𝔽_{r_j}^+ ⋊ C_{q^{e_j}} )
>
> acting on a partition n = k·p^α + r₁ + … + r_t, where the r_j are distinct primes, q^{e_j} | r_j − 1 for a **single common prime q**, d | p^α − 1, and the whole assembly fits one Oliver chain (bottom p-layer: the translation groups over p; middle cyclic layer: C_d and the 𝔽_{r_j}; top q-layer: the C_{q^{e_j}} twists). Consequently:
>
> - each block has size ≥ √(2M) > n^{3/4}, so the number of blocks is O(1) (indeed ≤ n^{1/4}, and ≤ 2–3 in extremal configurations);
> - intra-block orbitals are bounded by (block size) × (twist order), with twist order dividing (block size) − 1;
> - at most one block enjoys a general cyclic twist (via the middle layer); all others' twists are powers of the common q.

*Proof sketch and gap.* Block sizes ≥ √(2M) is trivial counting. The prime-power block claim: a transitive constituent of an Oliver group is solvable; for min-suborbit near-maximal the action approaches orbital-transitivity, where Zassenhaus's classification of sharply 2-transitive groups forces prime-power degree; in the sub-maximal but still-large regime, the solvable case of the affine-type classification (Γ ≤ AΓL(1, p^α)) applies to primitive constituents, and imprimitive towers degrade cross-orbitals at every level. **The unproven part is exactly this last clause**: rule out an imprimitive solvable tower (or an exotic non-affine primitive action) achieving min-orbital between n^{3/2} and n² on a non-prime-power domain. We believe imprimitivity is always taxed — each level of a block system caps some orbital by (number of superblocks) × (block-pair size) — but this needs a real argument. This is Open Problem 1 and everything downstream is conditional on it. The layer-assignment combinatorics (which twists can share which Oliver layer) also has enough cases to warrant careful verification.

Granting Claim 2.1, μ becomes a pure prime-representation function:

> **Corollary 2.2.** For 3/2 < 1 + θ ≤ 2: μ(n) ≥ n^{1+θ−o(1)} **iff** n = Σ bᵢ with O(1) prime-power summands bᵢ ≥ n^{3/4}, each carrying a divisor of bᵢ − 1 of size ≥ n^{1+θ}/bᵢ, q-coherently as in 2.1. (Proper prime powers have density zero and can be ignored in all heuristics.)

## 3. The provability barrier at 3/2

The BBKN/Shparlinski architecture fixes moduli first and invokes a least-prime-in-progression oracle: choose p, q, impose r ≡ 1 (mod q) and r ≡ n (mod p^α) — joint modulus D ≥ p^α q — and let the oracle supply a prime r in that class; the slack n − r is absorbed by replicating p^α-blocks (the count k is the free variable). Call this the **oracle architecture**.

> **Proposition 3.1 (barrier).** Under any least-prime oracle whatsoever — including false-strong ones — the oracle architecture yields m\* ≤ min{ n·p^α, q·r } with q ≤ r/p^α forced by r ≥ D ≥ p^α q (the trivial floor: r ≡ 1 mod q gives r > q; controlling r mod p^α costs the factor p^α). Balancing at p^α ~ √n gives m\* ≤ n^{3/2+o(1)}. Chowla's conjecture (least prime ≡ a mod D is O(D^{1+ε}); optimal, since it is ≫ φ(D) log D infinitely often) exactly saturates the architecture.

So the 3/2 of BBKN is the **exact ceiling of pointwise-progression number theory** fed into this template, and it is saturated: no strengthening of least-prime hypotheses moves it. Exceeding 3/2 requires *pinning* r to an exact value (r = n − m) and demanding primality plus a multiplicative side condition on r − 1 — a thin binary problem in the class of binary Goldbach, outside the implication range of ERH/GRH/Elliott–Halberstam and structurally inaccessible to sieves (parity problem + thin set). The barrier is epistemic, not ontological: the required statements are believed true.

*(Formalizing "oracle architecture" as a proof class — roughly, proofs whose arithmetic inputs quantify over all moduli/residues rather than over pinned integers — is Open Problem 4.)*

## 4. The conditional ladder to exponent 2

**Rung template.** n = m + r, both prime; Γ = AGL(1, m) × (𝔽_r ⋊ C_{q^e}) with q^e | r − 1. Oliver chain: 𝔽_m ◁ 𝔽_m ⋊ (C_{m−1} × 𝔽_r) ◁ Γ (middle cyclic since gcd(m−1, r) = 1). Orbitals: intra-m ~ m²/2 (2-transitive block), intra-r ~ q^e r/2, cross ≥ mr. With q^e ~ r^θ and m ~ r^{(1+θ)/2}:

> **Proposition 4.1 (conditional ladder).** For each θ ∈ (1/2, 1): if every large n is expressible as n = m + r with m, r prime and r − 1 having a prime-power divisor ~ r^θ, then μ(n) ≥ n^{1+θ−o(1)}. Each such hypothesis is a Hardy–Littlewood/Bateman–Horn-type prediction with expected representation count n^{1−o(1)}/polylog; each is believed; none is provable (θ = 0 already contains binary Goldbach). Small-residue adjustments of n (parity etc.) are handled by one auxiliary bounded block.

Notable near-miss: Baker–Harman-type theorems unconditionally give infinitely many primes r with a prime factor of r − 1 exceeding r^{0.67} — but those r are not pinned to Goldbach position n − m, and the pinning is exactly what exits provable territory, consistent with §3.

**Top rung.** e = 1, r a safe prime (r = 2q + 1):

> **Proposition 4.2.** Assume: every large n is expressible as n = m + 2q + 1 with m, q, 2q+1 all prime (a two-variable linear Hardy–Littlewood system; predicted count ~ c·n/log³n). Then, balancing m ~ r/√2, μ(n) ≥ n²/(2(1+√2)²) ≈ n²/11.7. Hence **all nontrivial monotone properties of graphs with ≤ δ₀n² edges are eventually evasive, δ₀ ≈ 0.085** — the full polynomial sparse regime, on one believed conjecture.

## 5. The terminal cap: a constant, by group theory

> **Proposition 5.1 (cap) ★.** There is a constant c\* < 1/2 such that for all non-prime-power n, μ(n) ≤ c\*·n². Reasons: (i) μ(n) = C(n,2) requires an orbital-transitive Oliver group, essentially sharply-2-transitive-type, which by Zassenhaus exists only on prime-power domains; (ii) with ≥ 2 blocks of sizes n₁ ≥ n₂, min{C(n₁,2)-scale intra, n₁n₂ cross, intra-2} cannot approach n²/2 under n₁ + n₂ ≤ n; (iii) the shared-layer constraint (one bottom prime, one top prime, one cyclic middle) permits only one fully-twisted (AGL-type) block, taxing the others. The precise value of c\* is an unfinished optimization (★).

So the method's dependence on number theory lies entirely in the believed-but-binary tier, and its true limit is group-theoretic — the same Zassenhaus classification that made the method arithmetic in the first place. Dense properties (beyond c\*n²) intrinsically require the other levers of the literature: recursion to quotient properties on the fixed-point complex (as in the forbidden-subgraph arguments of CKS/BBKN), or topological invariants finer than "fixed complex empty ⟹ χ = 0".

## 6. The converse: μ encodes prime distribution

Granting Claim 2.1, Corollary 2.2 is an equivalence, so lower bounds on μ *yield* additive prime theorems:

- **μ(n) = ω(n^{3/2}) for all large n ⟹** every large n is a sum of O(1) pinned prime powers with a large structured divisor of a shifted one — a theorem of binary-Goldbach difficulty. Any purely group-theoretic construction beating 3/2 would settle such problems as a corollary; the search problem does not route around the number theory, it **is** the number theory. This upgrades §3's barrier from "known techniques cap at 3/2" to "the object μ is itself equivalent, beyond 3/2, to statements beyond known techniques."
- **μ(n) = Θ(n²) for all n ⟺** the Goldbach-with-safe-primes statement of Prop 4.2 (necessity as well as sufficiency): the ladder is tight rung-by-rung, μ(n) ≥ n^{1+θ−o(1)} being equivalent to the θ-side-condition Goldbach variant.
- Refinement (speculative): counting extremal Γ at level M counts weighted representations, so μ's level sets in principle detect Hardy–Littlewood constants, modulo blurring by group-side equivalences.

Scope limit: μ probes exactly the Hardy–Littlewood/Bateman–Horn stratum (additive representations with multiplicative side conditions on shifted primes) — nothing about short intervals or L-function zeros except via that stratum.

## 7. Exact computations at small composite n

The asymptotic framework specializes, at each fixed composite n, to a finite computation. We implemented it (accompanying files: `oliver_mu.py`, `ark_intersect.py`, `run_logs.txt`).

**7.1 The template value μ_template(n).** Enumerating template Oliver groups (affine blocks with diagonal twists, prime-order block rotations, q-coherent middle twists; Oliver validity checked; u-orbitals by exact BFS) gives, e.g.: μ_template = 6/15 at n=6; 10/45 at n=10; 10/66 at n=12; 110/231 at n=22; 78/325 at n=26. Two phenomena predicted by the framework are visible exactly: (i) the constant is controlled by the arithmetic of the parts — n=22 nearly saturates the n²/2 wall because 11 is a safe prime, while n=12 and n=21 are the weak composites; (ii) a block-swap mechanism (fusing the two intra-block orbitals of 2×AGL(1,p) into a single orbital of doubled size) is the small-n workhorse — explicitly m\* = 110 at n=22 via 2×(𝔽₁₁⋊C₅) plus the swap.

**7.2 The intersection CSP at n=10.** Flipping the method into constraints: any non-evasive nontrivial monotone P must satisfy, for every Oliver Γ with top prime q, χ((P)_Γ) ≡ 1 mod q — with χ = 1 *exactly* when the top layer is trivial (q is then a free choice). Since orbitals are edge-disjoint, subsets of orbitals biject with union graphs; membership depends only on isomorphism class and is downward closed under subgraph embedding. We solved the joint CSP over all union-graph classes of a five-group battery at n=10 (two extremal m\*=10 groups, the block-swap group, the 5×2 rotation group, a 3+7 split). Result: **SAT with exactly 18 admissible membership patterns** on 18 iso classes. Backbone: the perfect matching is forced INTO any counterexample (the χ=1-exact condition of the ℤ₂⁵⋊C₅ rotation group admits no complex without it); the ≥40-edge classes are forced OUT. An enlarged eight-group battery gives 78 patterns on 32 classes (more groups add constraints *and* underdetermined classes; the per-pattern structure, not the count, is the progress metric).

**7.3 A disjunctive density theorem at n=10.** Reading the maximal forced-IN classes across all 18 patterns: **every admissible pattern contains at least one orbital-union class with ≥ 20 edges** (the classes occurring: 20 = K₅⊔K₅, 25 = K₅,₅-type cross unions, and 21-, 24-, 35-, 42-edge unions). Hence, unconditionally: *any counterexample to ARK at n=10 contains one of an explicit short list of dense vertex-transitive graphs on ≥ 20 of the 45 edges* — forced density 0.444 in disjunctive form, a 4× sharpening of the single-graph backbone bound. This is the exact small-n incarnation of the asymptotic prediction that counterexamples must be dense.

**7.4 The parity lever kills the minimal candidate.** The Zassenhaus-principled minimal candidate — P₀ = "max degree ≤ 1", the monotone Σₙ-closure of the forced backbone — is evasive by exact enumeration: χ(Δ_{P₀}) = Σₖ(−1)^{k−1}·mₖ(K₁₀) = −1215 ≠ 1 (mₖ = 1, 45, 630, 3150, 4725, 945), so the complex is far from contractible. Notably the topological lever cannot touch P₀ (a matching vacuously survives all large-orbital conditions) while the enumerative lever kills it instantly — a clean exhibit of the levers' complementary ranges.

**7.5 The parity screen completed (July 23).** We computed χ(Δ_{P_low}) *exactly* for the minimal completion of every pattern whose generators have component-decomposable closures, via three exact methods (each cross-validated): the exponential formula over signed connected-component weights — components with ≤ 7 vertices by complete enumeration of all 2.1M labeled graphs, bipartite components with 8–10 vertices by the two-sort EGF log(eˣ+eʸ−1), the two methods agreeing on every overlapping class; König-cover enumerations for vertex-cover-bounded families; and nauty/geng streams with pynauty automorphism orders for the α-threshold closures (the full 10-vertex stream read exactly 12,005,168 classes, matching the known count). Results: closure(K₅,₅) has χ = −288729; closure(K₅⊔K₅), −26424; closure(α≥5), 36541 (a signed sum over 10.16M K₅-free classes with billion-scale intermediate cancellation); closure(α≥3), −4424; closure(α≥7), −1721; and so on. **Nine of the eighteen patterns' minimal realizations are thereby proved evasive exactly** (p0–p2, p5–p8, p13–p14), with |χ| ranging from ~18,000 to ~933,000 — every computed complex is enormously far from the contractibility conspiracy χ = 1. No computed value came anywhere near 1, consistent with the "genericity of evasiveness" heuristic in its strongest quantitative form.

**7.6 The Smith-theory battery and the one-sidedness theorem-let.** Since non-evasiveness gives collapsibility, hence 𝔽ₚ-acyclicity for every p, Smith's theorem forces the fixed complex of *any* p-subgroup of Σₙ (not merely Oliver groups) to be 𝔽ₚ-acyclic — all reduced mod-p homology vanishing, and binding on *every* realization of a pattern, not just the minimal one. We ran this with ℤ₅² (𝔽₅) and ℤ₉ (𝔽₃) at n=10: **all 18 patterns survive**, and the mechanism of survival is the finding. Every surviving completion is a *cone*: an orbital O with U ∈ P ⟹ U∪O ∈ P contracts the fixed complex, and blocking every cone requires forcing unions OUT of P — but the patterns pin almost nothing on the OUT side (only K₁₀ and the ≥40-edge classes), so small orbitals cone freely below that ceiling. This exposes a structural fact about the entire method: Oliver's χ conditions, Smith acyclicity, and monotone propagation all push graphs *into* P; the sole OUT-generator in the toolkit is nontriviality. **The topological method is one-sided.** KSS succeeds at prime powers because a transitive group's orbital lattice is coarse enough that the IN-forcing and the single top OUT are jointly unsatisfiable; at composite n the lattices are fine, the top OUT cannot propagate down into the IN-forced region, and the candidate space lives precisely in the interval between. Closing composite n therefore requires a *down-forcing principle*: a derivation, from non-evasiveness, that specific dense-but-incomplete graphs lie outside P. One candidate exists: **complement duality** — P induces the dual monotone property on complements, non-evasiveness of P is equivalent to that of the dual, so the entire constraint battery applies to the dual complex, and the dual's forced-IN backbone is OUT-forcing on P. Running the dual battery and intersecting with the primal patterns is the identified next step; it is a mirror-image of the existing code.

**7.7 Status and the residual obstacle.** What remains at n=10: (i) nine patterns whose minimal completions involve the two *structural* closures — the lexicographic product C₅[K₂] (25 edges) and C₅⊔C₅ ∪ K₅,₅ (35 edges) — whose membership is not component-decomposable; their χ needs subgraph-class enumeration of these two fixed graphs (~10⁵–10⁶ classes each; a few hours with C-level nauty tooling); and (ii) the interval gap: killing P_low kills the *minimal* realization of a pattern, while denser realizations within each pattern's interval are untouched by any finite per-pattern computation — full unconditional closure of n=10 requires either CSP-UNSAT from an enlarged group battery or a theoretical argument covering intervals (e.g., showing χ is generically far from 1 on the whole interval, which the computed magnitudes make plausible but do not prove). n=12 — the first arithmetically weak composite, where the topological obstruction is weakest (10/66) — is the natural next target and the leading candidate location for a counterexample if one exists; the computation there is blocked only on subgraph-isomorphism engineering (dense middle-band VF2 instances; a labeled-containment shortcut for same-group classes removes most of the matrix).

## 8. Epistemic postscript (July 2026)

This note's expectations should be read against the events of May–July 2026: the falsification of the Erdős unit distance conjecture (via Golod–Shafarevich class field towers), of the Jacobian conjecture in C³, and of the Dinitz–Garg–Goemans cost conjecture — all three by or with AI-assisted search, all three having "survived decades of expert attention." Two transfers to ARK. First, survival-under-attention arguments are worth much less than previously assessed: they measured the human-search regime, which has ended. Second, the unit-distance case specifically shows that a falsifying mechanism can be principled, deep, and sitting for sixty years in an adjacent field — so the argument "every known source of structure fights the cancellation a counterexample needs" quantifies over *known* sources and should be discounted accordingly. Against this: ARK's support differs in kind from all three fallen conjectures (an identified partial mechanism with exactly-proven subclasses — prime-power n, bipartite properties, the sparse regime, minor-closed classes — rather than an extremal-optimality guess or an approximation-rounding wish). Net assessment: ARK as stated ~0.80 credence (down from ~0.90), with the failure mass concentrated at arithmetically weak composite n; weak evasiveness / Θ(n²) essentially unmoved at ≥ 0.97. Operationally: the small-n search of §7 should be run sincerely in both directions.

## 9. Open problems

1. **(Extremality ★, the keystone.)** Prove that among transitive solvable (Oliver-chain-compatible) groups of degree b, min-u-orbital exceeding b^{3/2}-scale forces affine type on prime-power degree; equivalently, rule out imprimitive solvable towers achieving intermediate exponents on non-prime-power domains. A counterexample would be independently interesting in permutation group theory and would localize a leak in the equivalence of §6 exactly where the interesting arithmetic lives. This feels finite and checkable, unlike the number theory.
2. **(Layer combinatorics.)** Verify the shared-layer analysis of Claim 2.1 (which twists can coexist in one Oliver chain) in full case detail; determine the exact extremal constant c\* of Prop 5.1 and the optimal δ₀ of Prop 4.2 (multi-block re-optimization may improve ≈1/12).
3. **(Number theory.)** Prove any rung of Prop 4.1 in an averaged or almost-all-n form. Shparlinski's method (linear equations in primes, Bombieri–Vinogradov inputs) unconditionalized the 5/4 rung; can averaging over n give "μ(n) ≥ n^{3/2+δ} for almost all n" unconditionally, in the spirit of BBKN's Theorem 1.3(b)? The obstruction: the side condition q^e | r − 1 is multiplicative on a pinned shift and may resist the exceptional-set dodge — determining whether it does is a well-posed analytic question.
4. **(Formalization.)** Make Prop 3.1's proof-class rigorous: define "certification by pointwise progression hypotheses" so that the 3/2 barrier becomes a theorem about a delimited class of arguments.
5. **(Small-n closure.)** (a) Compute χ for the two structural closures at n=10 (subgraphs of C₅[K₂] and of C₅⊔C₅ ∪ K₅,₅), finishing the minimal-completion screen; (b) run the **dual battery** (§7.6): compute the forced-IN backbone of the complement property's CSP and intersect with the primal patterns — the only identified source of down-forcing, and the only known route to interval-level kills; (c) run the full pipeline at n=12 and n=21, the arithmetically weak composites. Completing (b) to UNSAT would give the first unconditional ARK proof at a composite non-prime-power n beyond 6.
5′. **(Down-forcing in general.)** Determine whether any principle other than duality derives, from non-evasiveness of a monotone property at composite n, the exclusion of specific dense-but-incomplete graphs. The one-sidedness observation of §7.6 says the Oliver/Smith/enumerative arsenal has no such principle; whether duality alone suffices, and what its μ∪-style asymptotic invariant looks like (the joint forcing number over primal and dual batteries simultaneously), is open and is arguably the crispest question this project has produced.
6. **(Beyond the cap.)** For the regime above c\*n²: develop the recursion lever (fixed-point complex isomorphic to a smaller instance, as in the CKS/BBKN forbidden-subgraph machinery) or extract more than Euler characteristics from the fixed complex (equivariant/Smith-theoretic invariants), the only levers whose limiting resource is not the distribution of primes. Lutz's vertex-homogeneous contractible complexes show the group class itself cannot be enlarged beyond Oliver's.

## 10. One-paragraph summary

Abstracting BBKN, sparse evasiveness via orbital annihilation is governed by μ(n), the largest achievable minimum u-orbital over Oliver-condition groups on [n]. Conjecturally (modulo an extremality claim about solvable permutation groups), μ is *equivalent* to a graded family of additive prime-representation statements: the n^{3/2} exponent of BBKN is precisely the ceiling of pointwise least-prime-in-progression inputs (Chowla-saturated, hence final for that proof architecture), exponents in (3/2, 2] are equivalent to binary-Goldbach-type statements with multiplicative side conditions on shifted primes (believed, unprovable at present, with the top rung — Goldbach with safe primes — yielding evasiveness up to δ₀n² edges, δ₀ ≈ 1/12), and the method terminally caps at c\*n² for a group-theoretic constant c\* < 1/2 forced by Zassenhaus's classification, independent of any number theory. The group-search problem does not consume prime distribution results; it is a faithful group-theoretic shadow of them.
