--- RUN 6: Smith-theory (F_p-acyclicity) battery at n=10 (2026-07-23) ---

Basis: non-evasive => collapsible => F_p-acyclic for all p; Smith's theorem
then forces the fixed complex of ANY p-subgroup Q <= Sym(10) to be F_p-acyclic
(all reduced mod-p homology zero), far stronger than chi == 1 mod p.  Unlike
the chi screen, this constrains EVERY realization of a pattern, not just the
minimal one: a pattern with no admissible assignment dies globally.

Battery: Z5^2 (two 5-cycles; t=5, orbital sizes [5,5,5,5,25]; F_5) and
Z9 (9-cycle + fixed point; t=5, sizes [9,9,9,9,9]; F_3).  Homology routine
validated on simplex / triangle boundary / two points / edge.  Catalog grew
18 -> 36 classes.

RESULT: all 18 patterns SURVIVE -- for each, a monotone assignment of the new
classes exists making both fixed complexes F_p-acyclic.

DIAGNOSIS (the informative part): the surviving completions are CONES -- an
orbital O with U in P => U-union-O in P makes the fixed complex contractible.
Blocking every cone requires forcing unions OUT of P, and the patterns pin
almost nothing on the OUT side (only K_10 and the >=40-edge classes).  Small
orbitals cone freely below that ceiling.  Adding more small-orbital p-groups
cannot help for the same reason.

STRUCTURAL CONCLUSION: every constraint in the toolkit -- Oliver chi
conditions, Smith acyclicity, monotone propagation -- pushes graphs INTO P;
the only OUT-generator is nontriviality (K_10 not in P).  The topological
method is one-sided.  KSS wins at prime powers because a transitive group's
lattice is coarse enough that IN-forcing + the single top OUT are jointly
unsatisfiable; at composite n the lattice is fine, the top OUT cannot
propagate down to the IN-forced region, and all 18 patterns live in the
interval between.  Closing composite n requires a DOWN-FORCING principle:
a derivation, from non-evasiveness, that specific dense-but-incomplete graphs
lie outside P.

CANDIDATE (next step, "1-prime"): complement duality.  P monotone-decreasing
induces the dual property on complements; non-evasiveness of P is equivalent
to non-evasiveness of the dual, so the ENTIRE battery applies to the dual
complex, and the dual's forced-IN backbone is OUT-forcing on P -- exactly the
missing down-pressure.  Mirror-image of existing code; not yet run.

P^v = {G : complement(G) not in P} is monotone decreasing, nontrivial, and

the union of the complementary orbitals of the SAME group, the dual's
membership bits on each group's lattice are y[S] = 1 - x[comp(S)]: the dual
battery needs NO new variables.  Each Oliver group contributes a second
(complement-reflected) chi congruence; each Smith p-group a second
F_p-acyclicity condition on faces {S : x[comp(S)] = 0}.  Dual forced-INs are
primal forced-OUTs on dense graphs: the down-forcing missing in Run 6.

  projections onto original 18 classes .. all 18 original patterns SURVIVE

Diagnosis: the cone escape is SELF-DUAL.  A dual fixed complex is coned by an
orbital O* exactly when U not-in P => U-minus-O* ... i.e. when the primal's
sparse IN-side is weakly pinned, symmetric to Run 6's finding on the OUT side.
The pinned region (forced INs at <=5 edges, forced OUTs at >=40) leaves a
30-edge-wide free middle band, and complexes whose constrained faces all lie
outside the band's boundary can be contracted from either direction.  Duality
pincers the interval from both ends but the middle band is topologically
invisible to fixed-point conditions whose lattices are decoupled from the
pinned classes.
